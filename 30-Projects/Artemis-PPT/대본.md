## 대본 (수정본)

**[Introduction: 0:00 - 0:45]**
Imagine spending billions of dollars to return to a place humans visited over 50 years ago. That is exactly what NASA is doing with the Artemis II mission to the Moon. Recently, this mission broke the deep-space distance record set by the Apollo missions.
So, why are we going back to the Moon? Today, I will explain the real purpose behind this expensive mission and how it connects to humanity's future in space.

**[Body 1: 0:45 - 1:30]**
First, we are returning to collect valuable resources. The Moon is like a treasure chest. For example, scientists believe there is a large amount of water ice at the lunar South Pole. This ice could be used to make rocket fuel directly in space.
In addition, the Moon contains rare materials such as Helium-3, which may become a clean energy source in the future.

**[Body 2: 1:30 - 2:15]**
But resources are not the only reason. The Moon is also an important testing ground for space survival. Before humans travel to Mars, we need to practice living in space for long periods of time.
On the Moon, astronauts can test life-support systems and learn how to survive dangerous space radiation. Scientists can also study how to build long-term habitats for humans in deep space.

**[Body 3: 2:15 - 2:45]**  (수정: Lunar Gateway → 영구 기지)
Finally, the Moon mission is helping create a roadmap to Mars. The Moon can become a stepping stone for deeper space exploration. NASA plans to build a permanent lunar base, where astronauts could live and work directly on the Moon's surface.
In the future, this base could serve as a launching point for the first human mission to Mars.

**[Conclusion: 2:45 - 3:00]**
In conclusion, Artemis II is not simply a waste of money. It is an important investment in resources, technology, and the future of space exploration. This historic return to the Moon may become the first step toward making humanity a multi-planetary species. Thank you.
